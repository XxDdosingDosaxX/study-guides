Switch

IV → PO conversion

🔄

The highest-yield stewardship intervention on the ward

Switching to oral therapy is not a downgrade — for a stable patient with a bioavailable oral option it is the better treatment, because it removes a line. Every intravenous day carries phlebitis, thrombosis and bloodstream-infection risk that the oral route simply does not have. In the OVIVA trial of bone and joint infection, catheter complications occurred in 9.4% of the IV arm versus 1.0% of the oral arm — a number needed to treat of 12 to prevent one line complication, with no loss of efficacy.

The switch criteria — all must be satisfied

CriterionThresholdWhy
Haemodynamically stableNo vasopressors; blood pressure and heart rate stable without fluid bolusesShock deranges splanchnic perfusion and the volume of distribution — absorption becomes unpredictable.
AfebrileTemperature <38 °C for at least 24 hours (older protocols required 48 h or two consecutive readings)A health system that relaxed its trigger from 48 h to 24 h raised oral conversion from 38% to 64% and cut median length of stay from 4 days to 2.5 days with no change in readmission or mortality.
White count improvingTrending down and <20,000/µL; many modern protocols have dropped this criterion entirelyA single WBC value is a poor marker; the trend matters.
Clinically improvingSymptoms of the index infection resolving; no new deteriorationThe core judgement — everything else is a proxy.
Functioning gutEating or tolerating tube feeds; no vomiting, ileus, short-bowel syndrome, severe diarrhoea, NPO status, NG suction, or documented malabsorptionActive vomiting is a hard exclusion in every published protocol.
An adequate oral agent existsSufficient bioavailability and spectrum for this organism at this siteIf there is no oral drug that covers the bug at that site, there is no switch to make — this is the criterion most often skipped.
Syndromes where IV continues regardless of how well the patient looks: bacterial meningitis and other CNS infection · infective endocarditis (the POET trial's partial-oral strategy applies only to stabilised left-sided disease after ≥10 days of IV therapy, and is an ID decision) · S. aureus bacteraemia (the SABATO trial found oral step-down non-inferior, but only 4.2% of screened patients were eligible — this is not a floor decision) · profound neutropenia with mucositis · an undrained abscess or otherwise uncontrolled source · infected prosthetic material · severe sepsis or septic shock.

The preference order for oral step-down of Enterobacterales bacteraemia

  1. Ciprofloxacin 750 mg PO BID or levofloxacin 750 mg PO daily — highest and most reliable exposure.
  2. TMP-SMX 8–10 mg/kg/day of the trimethoprim component divided BID–TID (roughly 1 DS BID at 40–59 kg, 1 DS TID at 60–69 kg, 2 DS BID at 70–89 kg).
  3. β-lactams — cefuroxime 500 mg BID, amoxicillin 1,000 mg TID, cephalexin 500 mg QID, amox-clav 875 mg BID. Avoid entirely in ESBL or AmpC producers.
Monitor

Daily rounds & de-escalation

📈

The 48–72 hour antibiotic time-out

By 48–72 hours you have culture data, a clinical trajectory and a diagnosis you either believe or don't. This is the moment when broad empiric therapy either becomes targeted therapy or becomes an ongoing harm. Build the time-out into your daily note.

Empiric therapy is a hypothesis with a 48–72 hour expiry. If nothing changes at the time-out, the default is that the broadest possible regimen runs to completion — which is how resistance and C. difficile happen.

De-escalation — the specific moves

SituationMove
MRSA nasal PCR negative in pneumoniaStop vancomycin/linezolid. Negative predictive value ~96.5% overall and ~98.1% in community-acquired pneumonia. This alone saves an average of about a day of unnecessary vancomycin per patient.
Culture grows MSSAStop vancomycin; start cefazolin 2 g q8h or nafcillin 2 g q4h. Vancomycin is inferior for MSSA.
Cultures negative at 48–72 h in a patient who has improvedStop the empiric MRSA and antipseudomonal agents. Negative cultures in an improving patient are permission to narrow.
On pip-tazo or a carbapenem plus metronidazoleStop the metronidazole — the anaerobic coverage is already there. Redundant coverage is pure added toxicity.
Coagulase-negative staph in 1 of 4 bottles, patient well, no hardwareContaminant. Do not treat. Repeat cultures if genuinely uncertain.
Positive urine culture, no urinary symptomsAsymptomatic bacteriuria. Do not treat (except pregnancy or pre-urologic procedure). Look elsewhere for the cause of the delirium.
Sputum or wound culture growing an organism in a patient with no clinical infectionColonisation. Do not treat. Treat the patient, not the culture.
Two antipseudomonal agents, susceptibilities now backDrop to a single agent. Double coverage is for the empiric window only.
Stable, afebrile 24 h, eating, oral option existsSwitch to oral and plan discharge.
The harms are real and quantified. Roughly one in five hospitalised patients receiving antibiotics experiences an antibiotic-associated adverse event, and about one in five of those occurs in a patient whose antibiotic was not clinically indicated — an entirely avoidable harm. Add C. difficile, resistance selection, and an allergy label that will steer that patient toward worse antibiotics for the rest of their life. "Just in case" is not a benign default.
Escalate

When to broaden or call ICU

⬆️

Numeric triggers

Transfer to ICU for any of: vasopressor requirement to maintain MAP ≥65 after ≥30 mL/kg crystalloid · lactate >4 mmol/L, or failing to clear on adequate resuscitation · need for mechanical ventilation, or PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤250 with rising oxygen requirement · respiratory rate ≥30 with accessory muscle use or a rising PaCO₂ · GCS drop or new focal deficit · urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for >2 h despite fluids · arrhythmia with haemodynamic compromise · ≥3 ATS/IDSA minor severe-CAP criteria · any patient needing hourly nursing reassessment.
Trigger to broaden antibioticsWhat to add
Clinical deterioration on appropriate therapy with a controlled sourceConsider resistant gram-negatives — escalate to cefepime or a carbapenem; add MRSA cover if not already present.
Prior ESBL or CRE isolate anywhere in the last 12 monthsCarbapenem for ESBL; ceftazidime-avibactam or meropenem-vaborbactam for KPC-type CRE; involve ID.
New hypotension or lactate riseBroaden and re-image for source control. Broadening without re-imaging is the classic error.
Neutropenia developsAntipseudomonal β-lactam immediately; add vancomycin only for the specific indications.

Before you broaden, ask whether the problem is really the antibiotic. The differential for "not improving on antibiotics" is: undrained collection · retained infected hardware or line · wrong organism (fungal, mycobacterial, viral) · wrong diagnosis entirely (PE, drug fever, malignancy, crystal arthropathy, vasculitis, DVT masquerading as cellulitis) · inadequate dosing for the site or body size · malabsorption on an oral agent. Broadening fixes only one of those.
Dispo

Discharge, OPAT & disposition

🏠

Discharge readiness & where they go

Clinical stability (Halm criteria) — validated in pneumonia and a sound general framework: temperature ≤37.8 °C · heart rate ≤100 · respiratory rate ≤24 · systolic BP ≥90 mmHg · SaO₂ ≥90% or PaO₂ ≥60 mmHg on room air · able to take oral intake · normal mental status (or back to baseline).

Do not discharge on an abnormal vital sign because "they look fine." In a cohort of 3,984 pneumonia patients, 20.4% were discharged with at least one instability in the preceding 24 hours, and that was associated with an adjusted odds ratio of 3.70 for 30-day mortality. Notably, readmission was not significantly increased — these patients die rather than come back. Hypotension (aOR 4.51) and tachypnoea (aOR 3.13) were the strongest individual predictors. Conversely, once stability is achieved, subsequent deterioration requiring escalation occurs in ≤1% of cases.

Also required before discharge

  • Source control documented — the drain placed, the stone removed, the line pulled, the collection evacuated. Write it in the discharge summary.
  • A specific stop date on the prescription, not "until finished."
  • The patient can obtain and afford the drug. Linezolid, fidaxomicin and the newer agents can be prohibitively expensive — verify coverage before discharge, because an unfilled prescription is worse than an extra inpatient day.
  • Adherence-friendly dosing. Cefpodoxime BID beats cephalexin QID for a patient who will struggle with four doses a day; prefer the least frequent effective regimen.
  • Follow-up booked before the course ends, with explicit return precautions.
  • Allergy label reviewed — this admission is a delabelling opportunity.

OPAT — outpatient parenteral antimicrobial therapy

ElementDetail
WhoA stable patient needing prolonged parenteral therapy — endocarditis, osteomyelitis, complicated bacteraemia, CNS infection, resistant organisms with no oral option. Patients or caregivers may self-administer, with comparable safety to nurse-administered therapy. For people who inject drugs, the IDSA guideline makes no blanket exclusion — it is explicitly a case-by-case decision.
AccessPICC or tunnelled line for courses beyond ~2 weeks; a midline may suffice for shorter courses and non-vesicant drugs. Every line is a bloodstream-infection risk that must be justified.
MonitoringWeekly CBC and CMP as a baseline standard. Add vancomycin levels (AUC-guided), weekly CPK for daptomycin, LFTs for prolonged β-lactams and rifampin, and CBC weekly for linezolid and ceftaroline. A named physician must be responsible for reviewing the results.
Line-free alternatives — always ask firstDalbavancin 1500 mg IV ×1 or oritavancin 1200 mg IV ×1 for gram-positive SSTI — a single infusion and no line at all. Ceftriaxone 2 g IV daily or ertapenem 1 g IV daily for once-daily home therapy. Or an oral regimen — OVIVA for bone and joint infection and POET for stabilised endocarditis both showed oral non-inferiority in selected patients. The best line is no line.

Home

Stable, oral regimen, can self-manage, reliable follow-up within 48–72 h.

Home health

Home plus nursing for wound care, IV administration, or line management.

SNF

Needs 24-hour skilled nursing — complex wound care, IV therapy without home support, or deconditioning with <3 h therapy/day.

Acute rehab (IRF)

Can tolerate ≥3 h therapy/day across ≥2 disciplines — e.g. after debridement, amputation, or post-meningitis deficits.

LTAC

Prolonged complex care >25 days — ventilator weaning, extensive wounds, multi-week IV therapy with instability.

Hospice / palliative

Recurrent infection in advanced illness. Antibiotics are a legitimate comfort measure in some cases (dysuria, purulent secretions) and a burden in others — decide by goals, not reflex.

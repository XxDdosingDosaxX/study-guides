Hyperkalemia

On this page
Serum potassium above the reference range (>5.0–5.5 mmol/L) — a lab value that becomes a cardiac emergency because a rising extracellular K⁺ depolarizes the resting membrane until the heart's sodium channels stop conducting. The three moves never change: stabilize the membrane (calcium) → shift K⁺ into cells (insulin/albuterol) → remove K⁺ from the body (diuretic, binder, dialysis).
Mild 5.5–5.9 · Moderate 6.0–6.4 · Severe ≥6.5 Get an ECG at K ≥6.0 or any symptom Emergency: ECG changes or K ≥6.5 Anchor: Ca gluconate 1 g IV → insulin 10 u + D50 Shift is temporary — recheck K in 1–2 h

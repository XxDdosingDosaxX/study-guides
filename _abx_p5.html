Coverage by pathogen

🦠

Read it backwards — "the culture grew X, now what?"

The spectrum grid answers "what does this drug cover?" This table answers the question you actually get paged about: "the blood cultures are growing X — is my patient on the right antibiotic?" The drug of choice is listed first; resistance figures are US national estimates and will differ from your hospital's antibiogram, which always wins.

Gram-positive cocci in clusters — Staphylococcus. Clusters mean staph; your next decision is MSSA vs MRSA and whether this is a contaminant (coagulase-negative in 1 of 4 bottles) or the real thing.
__CREDIT_gram_pos_cocci__
Gram-positive lancet-shaped diplococci — Streptococcus pneumoniae. Pairs and chains mean strep; in a CAP sputum or CSF this is your answer.
__CREDIT_gram_strep__
Gram-negative rods — E. coli and the rest of the Enterobacterales. In a septic patient this is the finding that makes you ask: does this patient need Pseudomonas or ESBL coverage?

MSSA
methicillin-susceptible S. aureus		Cefazolin 2 g IV q8h or nafcillin/oxacillin 2 g IV q4h. PO step-down: dicloxacillin 500 mg QID or cephalexin 500 mg QID/1 g TID.Vancomycin (inferior — higher failure and mortality in MSSA bacteraemia); ceftriaxone (works but is broader and less potent than cefazolin); ampicillin/penicillin (>90% of S. aureus makes penicillinase).A large share of US S. aureus remains methicillin-susceptible — CDC/NHSN reports methicillin resistance in 45.5% of device-associated healthcare infections and 39.2% of surgical-site infections, so most isolates in many settings are MSSA. The moment susceptibilities return MSSA, de-escalate off vancomycin — this single move measurably improves outcomes.
MRSAVancomycin (AUC 400–600) · daptomycin 6–10 mg/kg (not lungs) · linezolid 600 mg q12h (best lung penetration, oral option) · ceftaroline 600 mg q12h. Oral for SSTI: TMP-SMX, doxycycline, clindamycin.Every penicillin and cephalosporin except ceftaroline — mecA/PBP2a defeats them all. Adding a β-lactamase inhibitor does nothing.There is no single valid national MRSA percentage — it depends entirely on the setting and specimen. CDC/NHSN reports methicillin resistance in 45.5% of device-associated healthcare infections and 39.2% of surgical-site infections; community rates differ again. Use your own antibiogram. Community-associated MRSA is usually clindamycin/TMP-SMX/doxycycline-susceptible; healthcare-associated strains are more multi-drug resistant. Check the D-test before using clindamycin.
Streptococcus pyogenes
Group A strep		Penicillin (G 2–4 MU IV q4h; VK 500 mg PO QID) or amoxicillin 500 mg TID ×10 d. Add clindamycin 900 mg IV q8h for toxic shock/necrotising infection.TMP-SMX and ciprofloxacin — both unreliable for Group A strep. Clindamycin is added not for killing but for ribosomal toxin suppression (the Eagle effect: in a high-inoculum stationary-phase infection, β-lactams lose potency because the organisms are not dividing).
Streptococcus pneumoniaeCeftriaxone 1–2 g IV q24h (2 g q12h for meningitis) or high-dose amoxicillin 1 g PO TID. Alternatives: respiratory fluoroquinolone, ceftaroline.Azithromycin monotherapy in a comorbid patient — about 25% of invasive US pneumococci are erythromycin-resistant (CDC ABCs 2023). Ciprofloxacin (no pneumococcal activity). TMP-SMX (~20% non-susceptible).Penicillin non-susceptibility is a PBP-affinity change, not a β-lactamase — so higher doses overcome it (a key reason CAP amoxicillin dosing is 1 g TID, not 500 mg TID). CSF breakpoints are far stricter than non-CNS breakpoints, which is why meningitis empiric therapy adds vancomycin.
Viridans group strepPenicillin G 12–18 MU/day or ceftriaxone 2 g IV q24h ×4 weeks for endocarditis (2 weeks if combined with gentamicin and the strain is highly susceptible).—Increasingly penicillin-non-susceptible, particularly in neutropenic patients, where viridans strep can cause fulminant sepsis and ARDS. Vancomycin for the resistant or allergic.
Enterococcus faecalisAmpicillin 2 g IV q4h (the drug of choice). Endocarditis: ampicillin + ceftriaxone 2 g q12h. Alternatives: penicillin G, vancomycin.All cephalosporins (intrinsic resistance — enterococci have low-affinity PBP5), TMP-SMX (works in vitro, fails in vivo because enterococci scavenge exogenous folate), clindamycin, aminoglycosides alone.Ampicillin-susceptibility is the rule for faecalis. The ampicillin + ceftriaxone double-β-lactam regimen has replaced ampicillin + gentamicin for endocarditis — equal efficacy with far less nephrotoxicity.
Enterococcus faecium / VRELinezolid 600 mg IV/PO q12h or daptomycin 8–12 mg/kg (higher doses used for VRE bacteraemia).Ampicillin (usually resistant in faecium), vancomycin (vanA/vanB change the target to D-Ala-D-Lac), all cephalosporins.Distinguish the species — faecalis is usually ampicillin-susceptible; faecium usually is not. VRE in urine may be treatable with nitrofurantoin or fosfomycin. Often colonisation rather than infection: do not treat a VRE-positive rectal swab or a contaminated wound culture.
Listeria monocytogenesAmpicillin 2 g IV q4h (± gentamicin for synergy in severe disease). Penicillin-allergic: TMP-SMX 10–20 mg/kg/day of TMP divided q6–12h.All cephalosporins — this is why meningitis empiric therapy in anyone ≥50, pregnant, alcohol-dependent, or immunocompromised must include ampicillin. Vancomycin is also unreliable.Think Listeria in the elderly, pregnant, and T-cell-immunosuppressed (steroids, transplant, TNF inhibitors). Presents with meningitis or rhombencephalitis and a lymphocyte-predominant or mixed CSF.
Haemolysis on blood agar. Complete (β) haemolysis clears the agar — S. pyogenes, S. agalactiae. Partial (α) haemolysis leaves a green halo — pneumococcus and viridans strep.

Escherichia coliSusceptibility-driven. Cystitis: nitrofurantoin 100 mg BID ×5 d, TMP-SMX DS BID ×3 d, or fosfomycin 3 g ×1. Systemic: ceftriaxone 1–2 g q24h; step down to cefpodoxime/amox-clav/TMP-SMX by susceptibility.Ampicillin or amoxicillin alone — pooled penicillin-class resistance in US outpatient urinary isolates is around 43%. First-generation cephalosporins are unreliable for systemic infection.In the largest US dataset (1.5 million outpatient female urine isolates, 2011–2019), E. coli was non-susceptible to TMP-SMX in 25.4% and to fluoroquinolones in 21.1%, versus only 3.8% for nitrofurantoin — and ESBL prevalence is rising about 8% relatively per year. IDSA advises avoiding TMP-SMX empirically when local resistance exceeds 20%, and adding a single long-acting parenteral dose to an oral fluoroquinolone for pyelonephritis when local FQ resistance exceeds 10%.
Klebsiella pneumoniaeCeftriaxone or cefepime by susceptibility; carbapenem if ESBL.Ampicillin — intrinsically resistant (chromosomal SHV β-lactamase). Nitrofurantoin is unreliable.A leading ESBL and carbapenemase (KPC) producer. Hypervirulent strains (string-test-positive, often K. pneumoniae K1/K2) cause liver abscess with metastatic endophthalmitis, classically in patients of East Asian descent with diabetes.
Proteus mirabilisAmpicillin, ceftriaxone, TMP-SMX, or a fluoroquinolone by susceptibility.Nitrofurantoin — intrinsically resistant. Tetracyclines and colistin also fail.Urease-producer → alkaline urine and struvite (staghorn) stones. A urine pH >7 with a UTI should make you think Proteus and get imaging.
Enterobacter cloacae complex · Klebsiella aerogenes · Citrobacter freundii
the AmpC organisms (IDSA 2024)		Cefepime 2 g IV q8h or a carbapenem. Non-β-lactam options: TMP-SMX, fluoroquinolone by susceptibility.Ceftriaxone and other 3rd-generation cephalosporins — even when reported susceptible. Also all penicillins and 1st/2nd-generation cephalosporins.These carry an inducible chromosomal AmpC β-lactamase. Exposure to a 3rd-generation cephalosporin selects de-repressed mutants and the patient relapses mid-course with a resistant organism. Cefepime is stable because it is a poor AmpC substrate and penetrates fast. IDSA 2024 narrowed the list to the three organisms at high enough risk of clinically significant AmpC production to matter: Enterobacter cloacae complex, Klebsiella aerogenes and Citrobacter freundii. The older mnemonics "SPICE" and "HECK-Yes" are still widely taught but are obsolete and over-inclusive — "indole-positive Proteus" in particular is now considered wrong. Learn the three organisms rather than the mnemonic.
Pseudomonas aeruginosaCefepime 2 g q8h · pip-tazo 4.5 g q6h (or extended infusion) · meropenem 1–2 g q8h · ceftazidime 2 g q8h · ciprofloxacin 400 mg IV q8h / 750 mg PO BID (the only reliable oral option) · aztreonam in severe β-lactam allergy. MDR: ceftolozane-tazobactam, ceftazidime-avibactam, cefiderocol.Ceftriaxone, ertapenem, ampicillin-sulbactam, moxifloxacin — all commonly assumed to cover Pseudomonas and none of them do. Also all oral cephalosporins.Risk factors that should make you cover it empirically: structural lung disease (bronchiectasis, cystic fibrosis, advanced COPD on steroids), neutropenia, recent broad-spectrum antibiotics or hospitalisation, prior Pseudomonas isolation, burns, ventilator, indwelling devices. Combines all four resistance mechanisms — it can become resistant during therapy, so re-culture if the patient is not improving.
ESBL producers
usually CTX-M in E. coli/Klebsiella		A carbapenem — meropenem 1 g q8h for severe infection, ertapenem 1 g q24h for stable patients and OPAT. Non-bacteraemic cystitis: nitrofurantoin, fosfomycin, or an aminoglycoside.Piperacillin-tazobactam for bloodstream infection (the MERINO trial found 30-day mortality 12.3% with pip-tazo vs 3.7% with meropenem and failed non-inferiority). Cefepime is also unreliable. All 3rd-generation cephalosporins.ESBLs hydrolyse penicillins, all cephalosporins and aztreonam, but not carbapenems or cephamycins. Risk factors: prior ESBL, recent β-lactam or fluoroquinolone exposure, healthcare exposure, travel to high-prevalence regions, long-term care residence.
CRE
carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales		KPC (class A): ceftazidime-avibactam 2.5 g q8h or meropenem-vaborbactam 4 g q8h. Metallo-β-lactamase (NDM/VIM/IMP): cefiderocol, or ceftazidime-avibactam plus aztreonam. OXA-48: ceftazidime-avibactam.Ceftazidime-avibactam and meropenem-vaborbactam against metallo-β-lactamases — a common and lethal mistake. Ertapenem. Tigecycline for bacteraemia.Always identify the carbapenemase — the mechanism dictates the drug, and the epidemiology is shifting fast. In CDC Emerging Infections Program surveillance, NDM rose from 5.4% of carbapenemase-producing CRE in 2016 to 39.8% in 2023, while KPC fell from 92.8% to 53.0%. Since avibactam and vaborbactam do not touch metallo-β-lactamases, "CRE → give ceftazidime-avibactam" is no longer a safe reflex. Involve ID early. Colistin is a last resort with real nephrotoxicity.
Acinetobacter baumanniiAmpicillin-sulbactam (high dose — sulbactam has intrinsic activity) or a carbapenem; MDR: cefiderocol, high-dose sulbactam-durlobactam, polymyxins, minocycline.Ertapenem, aztreonam, ceftriaxone.Almost always ICU-acquired, often ventilator-associated; frequently colonisation rather than infection — treat the patient, not the sputum.
Stenotrophomonas maltophiliaTMP-SMX (15 mg/kg/day of TMP divided) is the drug of choice. Alternatives: levofloxacin, minocycline, ceftazidime-avibactam + aztreonam.All carbapenems — intrinsically resistant (it makes a metallo-β-lactamase). Most β-lactams.Classically emerges because the patient has been on a carbapenem. A gram-negative rod appearing on day 10 of meropenem is Stenotrophomonas until proven otherwise.
Haemophilus influenzaeAmoxicillin-clavulanate, 2nd/3rd-generation cephalosporin, azithromycin, doxycycline, or a respiratory fluoroquinolone.Plain amoxicillin/ampicillin in β-lactamase-producing strains (roughly a quarter to a third of isolates).A key CAP and COPD-exacerbation pathogen, especially in smokers. Non-typeable strains are not prevented by the Hib vaccine.
Moraxella catarrhalisAmox-clav, cefuroxime, azithromycin, doxycycline, TMP-SMX.Plain amoxicillin — nearly all isolates produce β-lactamase.Sinusitis, otitis, COPD exacerbation.
Neisseria meningitidisCeftriaxone 2 g IV q12h (or penicillin G 4 MU q4h if susceptible) ×7 days. Prophylaxis for close contacts: ciprofloxacin 500 mg PO ×1, rifampin 600 mg BID ×2 d, or ceftriaxone 250 mg IM ×1.Vancomycin.Droplet precautions for 24 h. Check terminal complement (C5–C9) function in recurrent or unusual serogroup disease, and in anyone on eculizumab/ravulizumab.
Neisseria gonorrhoeaeCeftriaxone 500 mg IM ×1 (1 g if ≥150 kg); treat for chlamydia with doxycycline 100 mg BID ×7 d unless excluded.Fluoroquinolones (widespread resistance — no longer recommended), oral cefixime (second-line only), azithromycin monotherapy.Resistance is escalating globally; CDC now recommends ceftriaxone monotherapy at the higher dose rather than the old dual-therapy approach.

Reading the micro lab — susceptibility, MIC, and what "S" actually means

The susceptibility report is not a ranking of drug strength. S / I / R are site- and dose-specific predictions made by comparing the organism's MIC to a breakpoint that assumes a particular drug, dose and body site. A pneumococcus reported "penicillin-susceptible" for a lung infection may be "penicillin-resistant" by CSF breakpoints for the same MIC — the bug did not change; the achievable concentration at the site did.

Disk diffusion (Kirby-Bauer) — a susceptible isolate. Antibiotic diffuses out of each paper disk into the agar; a wide clear zone means growth was inhibited at achievable concentrations.
__CREDIT_disk_diffusion__
The same test, a multidrug-resistant isolate. Bacteria grow right up to the disks — no zone, no activity. This is what an ESBL or MDR Pseudomonas looks like on a plate.
__CREDIT_disk_diffusion_resistant__
Gradient strip (Etest) — reading an MIC. The strip carries a continuous antibiotic gradient; you read the MIC where the elliptical growth margin intersects the scale. This is how a vancomycin or oxacillin MIC gets onto your patient's chart.
__CREDIT_etest_mic__
The same technique, whole-plate view — S. pneumoniae tested against cefotaxime. The teardrop of inhibited growth narrows toward the strip; where its edge meets the scale is the MIC that the lab compares against the breakpoint.
__CREDIT_etest_plate__
ESBL confirmation. Ceftazidime alone versus ceftazidime + clavulanate: a ≥5 mm zone expansion when the inhibitor is added proves the resistance is β-lactamase-mediated. This is the test behind the word "ESBL" on your report.

What the report saysWhat it actually meansWhat you do
MIC (minimum inhibitory concentration)The lowest drug concentration that stops visible growth in vitro. It is a property of the organism–drug pair, not a measure of how good the drug is.Never compare MICs between different drugs — a vancomycin MIC of 1 and a cefazolin MIC of 1 mean completely different things. Compare only against that drug's breakpoint.
S — susceptibleStandard dosing should achieve concentrations at the infection site that exceed the MIC for long enough to work.Use it — and prefer the narrowest S agent on the panel.
I — intermediate / "susceptible-dose-dependent"Achievable only with maximal dosing or where the drug concentrates (urine, bile).Use only at maximum dose and only if the site concentrates the drug; otherwise choose another agent.
R — resistantPredicted failure at any safe dose.Do not use, regardless of how well the patient seems to be doing.
Suppressed / not reportedLabs deliberately hide broad-spectrum results (cascade reporting) to steer you to the narrow agent, and hide agents known to fail in vivo.Call the micro lab if you genuinely need the hidden result — don't assume the drug wasn't tested.
Oxacillin R on a staph panelThis is the MRSA readout — mecA/PBP2a. Oxacillin is the surrogate tested for all β-lactams.Every β-lactam fails except ceftaroline, irrespective of what other β-lactam MICs appear.
Erythromycin R, clindamycin SPossible inducible erm-mediated resistance.Ask for the D-test. If positive, clindamycin will fail on therapy — pick something else.
Get the cultures before the first dose — but never delay antibiotics in septic shock to get them. Two sets of blood cultures from two separate sites before antibiotics roughly doubles the yield and is what lets you de-escalate on day 3. A single positive coagulase-negative staph bottle out of four is contamination; the same organism in both sets, or in a patient with prosthetic material, is not.

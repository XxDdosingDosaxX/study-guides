# HEART FAILURE — Comprehensive Clinical Reference (Guideline-Verified)

**Primary sources:** 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA Guideline for the Management of Heart Failure ([Circulation](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001063) / [JACC](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2021.12.012)), 2021 Universal Definition and Classification of HF ([J Card Fail](https://onlinejcf.com/article/S1071-9164(21)00050-6/fulltext)), 2022 SCAI SHOCK Stages update ([JACC](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2022.04.049)), 2023 ESC Focused Update ([ACC summary](https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/ten-points-to-remember/2023/08/29/14/58/2023-focused-update-esc-guidelines-hf-esc-2023)), and primary trials cited throughout. Class of Recommendation (COR) / Level of Evidence (LOE) given where stated in the 2022 guideline.

## 1. Classification & Definitions

### Universal Definition of HF (2021 Consensus)
HF = **a clinical syndrome with symptoms and/or signs caused by a structural and/or functional cardiac abnormality, corroborated by elevated natriuretic peptides AND/OR objective evidence of pulmonary or systemic congestion** ([Universal Definition, J Card Fail 2021](https://onlinejcf.com/article/S1071-9164(21)00050-6/fulltext); [ACC summary](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2021/07/12/12/31/universal-definition-and-classification-of-heart-failure)).

| Category | LVEF criterion |
|---|---|
| **HFrEF** (reduced) | **LVEF ≤40%** |
| **HFmrEF** (mildly reduced) | **LVEF 41–49%** |
| **HFpEF** (preserved) | **LVEF ≥50%** |
| **HFimpEF** (improved) | **Baseline LVEF ≤40% + ≥10-point increase + second measurement >40%** |

**Critical HFimpEF rule:** GDMT must be **continued indefinitely** even after EF recovers — in **TRED-HF**, withdrawal of therapy in recovered dilated cardiomyopathy led to relapse in ~40% within 6 months (2022 guideline, Class 1 to continue GDMT in HFimpEF).

### NYHA Functional Class (symptoms, changeable)
- **I** — No limitation; ordinary activity causes no symptoms
- **II** — Slight limitation; comfortable at rest, ordinary activity causes symptoms
- **III** — Marked limitation; comfortable at rest, less-than-ordinary activity causes symptoms
- **IV** — Symptoms at rest or with any activity

### ACC/AHA Stages (progressive, never move backward)
- **A — At risk:** HTN, DM, obesity, CAD, cardiotoxins, family history; no structural disease, no symptoms
- **B — Pre-HF:** Structural disease (↓EF, LVH, chamber enlargement, wall motion abnormality), elevated filling pressures, or elevated NP — **without symptoms**
- **C — Symptomatic HF:** Structural disease + current or prior symptoms
- **D — Advanced HF:** Refractory symptoms despite GDMT; recurrent hospitalizations; candidates for transplant/LVAD/inotropes/hospice

### Cardiogenic Shock
Classic definition: **SBP <90 mmHg for ≥30 min (or need for vasopressors/inotropes to maintain SBP ≥90)** + evidence of end-organ hypoperfusion (lactate >2 mmol/L, urine output <30 mL/h, cool extremities, altered mentation) + adequate or elevated filling pressures. Hemodynamic criteria: **cardiac index <2.2 L/min/m², PCWP >15 mmHg**. Cardiac power output <0.6 W predicts mortality.

### SCAI SHOCK Stages A–E (2022 update)
([SCAI/JACC 2022](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2022.04.049); [SCAI bedside checklist](https://www.scai.org/sites/default/files/2022-04/SCAI%20SHOCK%20Bedside%20Checklist%202022.pdf))

| Stage | Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | At risk | Large AMI or acute-on-chronic HF; no hypotension/hypoperfusion |
| **B** | Beginning | Hypotension (SBP <90 or MAP <60) or tachycardia **without hypoperfusion**; lactate <2 |
| **C** | Classic | Hypoperfusion requiring intervention (inotrope/pressor/MCS) beyond volume; lactate ≥2, ↑Cr, cool/mottled |
| **D** | Deteriorating | Failing to stabilize despite initial intensification (escalating pressors/MCS) |
| **E** | Extremis | Actual/impending circulatory collapse; refractory hypotension/arrest, CPR, pH ≤7.2, lactate ≥8 |

Cardiac arrest is a modifier at any stage. Observed mortality gradient C→E: ~22% → ~70% ([CCCTN registry, Circ HF](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIRCHEARTFAILURE.122.009714)).

## 2. Pathophysiology (the "why" behind each drug)

### The neurohormonal cascade
1. **Index insult** (MI, HTN, valve disease, cardiomyopathy) → ↓cardiac output → ↓effective arterial volume sensed by baroreceptors and juxtaglomerular apparatus.
2. **Sympathetic activation:** ↑NE → ↑HR, ↑contractility, vasoconstriction. Short-term rescue → long-term **myocyte toxicity, apoptosis, β-receptor downregulation, arrhythmia**.
3. **RAAS activation:** renal hypoperfusion → renin → angiotensin II (vasoconstriction, ↑afterload) → **aldosterone** (Na/water retention, ↑preload, **myocardial/vascular fibrosis**).
4. **Natriuretic peptides (ANP/BNP)** released from stretched myocardium — counter-regulatory (natriuresis, vasodilation, anti-fibrotic) but overwhelmed; **degraded by neprilysin** (the therapeutic target of ARNI).
5. **Cardiac remodeling:** eccentric hypertrophy, chamber dilation, ↑wall stress (Laplace), mitral regurgitation → a self-perpetuating maladaptive spiral.

**Frank-Starling & compensation gone bad:** Increased preload initially augments stroke volume, but the failing ventricle operates on a flattened Starling curve — further volume only raises filling pressures → congestion. Increased afterload (RAAS/SNS vasoconstriction) further depresses stroke volume in the failing heart.

### Each GDMT pillar tied to mechanism
| Drug class | Mechanism interrupted |
|---|---|
| **ARNI** (sacubitril/valsartan) | Neprilysin inhibition **augments protective natriuretic peptides** + AT1 blockade blocks angiotensin II. Dual hit on both maladaptive and counter-regulatory arms. |
| **ACEi / ARB** | Block RAAS: ↓angiotensin II vasoconstriction, ↓afterload, ↓aldosterone, ↓remodeling. (ACEi also ↑bradykinin → cough.) |
| **Beta-blocker** | Block chronic sympathetic toxicity → ↓HR (↑diastolic filling/coronary perfusion), anti-arrhythmic, reverse remodeling, ↑EF over months. |
| **MRA** | Block aldosterone → ↓Na/water retention AND **↓myocardial/vascular fibrosis** (key anti-remodeling effect independent of diuresis). |
| **SGLT2i** | Glucose-independent: natriuresis/osmotic diuresis, ↓preload/afterload, improved cardiac energetics (ketone utilization), ↓inflammation/fibrosis, ↑hematocrit. |

### HFpEF pathophysiology (distinct from HFrEF)
- **Diastolic dysfunction:** impaired myocardial relaxation + ↑passive stiffness (concentric LVH, interstitial fibrosis, titin modification) → normal EF but ↑LV filling pressures.
- Driven by systemic inflammation from comorbidities (HTN, obesity, DM, CKD, aging) → coronary microvascular endothelial dysfunction → myocardial fibrosis/stiffening. Small stiff ventricle is **preload-dependent and intolerant of tachycardia/AF** (loss of atrial kick, shortened diastole). This is why HFrEF neurohormonal drugs largely failed in HFpEF — the primary lesion is stiffness/microvascular inflammation, not systolic RAAS/SNS-driven remodeling.

## 3. GDMT for HFrEF — the "Four Pillars"

**Foundational quadruple therapy** (2022 guideline, all Class 1 for HFrEF): (1) **ARNI/ACEi/ARB**, (2) **evidence-based beta-blocker**, (3) **MRA**, (4) **SGLT2i**. Goal is to have all four classes on board (each at lowest effective/tolerated dose) rather than maximizing one before starting the next — full quadruple therapy reduces 2-year mortality substantially vs partial therapy.

| Enalapril | 2.5 mg BID | 10–20 mg BID |

**ARNI key rules:**
- **36-hour washout** required between last ACEi dose and first ARNI dose (avoid additive bradykinin → angioedema). No washout needed switching from an ARB.
- Preferred over ACEi/ARB (Class 1) in chronic symptomatic HFrEF; in-hospital initiation in stabilized de novo/decompensated HFrEF is reasonable (PIONEER-HF).

**MRA initiation/monitoring thresholds (2022 guideline):**
- Initiate only if **eGFR >30 mL/min/1.73m²** and **K⁺ <5.0 mEq/L**.
- Recheck K⁺ and renal function within 2–3 days and at 7 days after start, then periodically.
- **Discontinue if K⁺ cannot be maintained <5.5 mEq/L.** Contraindicated at eGFR ≤30 or K⁺ ≥5.0 at baseline.

### Loop diuretics for congestion (symptom control, no mortality benefit)
| Drug | Oral→IV | Approx. equivalence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Furosemide | 40 mg PO = **20 mg IV** (≈50% oral bioavailability) | 40 mg | Bioavailability erratic in gut edema |
| Bumetanide | 1 mg PO ≈ 1 mg IV | 1 mg ≈ 40 mg furosemide | More reliable oral absorption |
| Torsemide | 20 mg PO ≈ 20 mg IV | 20 mg ≈ 40 mg furosemide | Best/most predictable oral bioavailability (~80–100%) |

- **TRANSFORM-HF** (2023): torsemide vs furosemide after HF hospitalization — **no difference** in all-cause mortality or QoL over 12 mo ([Circulation](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.123.064842)).

### Second-line / additional therapies
- **Hydralazine + ISDN:** Class 1 for **self-identified Black patients** with NYHA III–IV HFrEF on optimal GDMT (**A-HeFT** — 43% relative mortality reduction, 6.2% vs 10.2%, HR 0.57; [NEJM 2004](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa042934)). Class 2b in others who cannot tolerate ACEi/ARB/ARNI.
- **Ivabradine (SHIFT):** Class 2a for **sinus rhythm, HR ≥70 bpm, LVEF ≤35%, NYHA II–III on maximally tolerated beta-blocker.** Selective I_f (SA node) inhibitor — HR reduction only, no effect on contractility/BP. NOT effective in AF.
- **Digoxin:** Class 2b — reduces hospitalizations, no mortality benefit (DIG trial). Target level 0.5–0.9 ng/mL; toxicity risk with renal failure, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia.
- **Vericiguat (VICTORIA):** Class 2b — oral soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator for **EF <45% with a recent worsening HF event (hospitalization or IV diuretics <6 mo)** ([VICTORIA design/PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8977472/)).
- **Device therapy referral:**
  - **ICD (primary prevention, Class 1):** LVEF **≤35%**, NYHA II–III on GDMT ≥3 months, expected survival >1 year; **ischemic** cardiomyopathy also qualifies at LVEF ≤30%. Wait **≥40 days post-MI** and **≥90 days on GDMT** before reassessing EF for implantation.
  - **CRT (Class 1):** LVEF **≤35%**, **sinus rhythm, LBBB with QRS ≥150 ms**, NYHA II–IV (ambulatory) on GDMT. Weaker (Class 2a/2b) for non-LBBB or QRS 120–149 ms.

### Contraindications, side effects & alternatives
| Class | Key adverse effects | If intolerant/contraindicated |
|---|---|---|
| ACEi | Cough (bradykinin), angioedema, hyperkalemia, ↑Cr, teratogenic | **ACEi cough → switch to ARB**; ideally ARNI |
| ARB | Hyperkalemia, ↑Cr, teratogenic (no cough) | ARNI, or hydralazine/ISDN if RAAS not tolerated |
| ARNI | Hypotension, hyperkalemia, angioedema (never combine with/within 36h of ACEi) | ARB or ACEi |
| Beta-blocker | Bradycardia, hypotension, fatigue, mask hypoglycemia; avoid initiating in decompensated/wet patients | Switch among the 3 proven agents; do not stop abruptly |
| MRA | **Hyperkalemia**, ↑Cr, gynecomastia (spironolactone) | Gynecomastia → **eplerenone**; hyperkalemia → K-binder (patiromer/SZC) or reduce dose |
| SGLT2i | Genital mycotic infections, volume depletion, rare **euglycemic DKA** (hold periop/acute illness), Fournier gangrene | Other SGLT2i; few true alternatives |
| Hydralazine/ISDN | Headache, hypotension, reflex tachycardia, drug-induced lupus (hydralazine) | Dose reduction |

### Sequencing / speed of titration
- Modern approach: initiate all 4 pillars rapidly (often within days, even in-hospital), then up-titrate — not the old "one at a time to max" model.
- **STRONG-HF** ([Lancet 2022](https://www.thelancet.com/); [tctmd](https://www.tctmd.com/news/strong-hf-rapid-uptitration-gdmt-after-acute-hf-pays-dividends)): rapid up-titration to full GDMT within ~2 weeks of discharge + close follow-up (labs/exam at 1,2,3,6 wk) → **absolute risk reduction 8.1%** for 180-day death/HF readmission (RR 0.66, 95% CI 0.50–0.86, p=0.0021). Safe, greater QoL. Practice-changing mandate for aggressive early optimization.

## 4. HFpEF Management

**What has evidence:**
- **SGLT2i — Class 1 (2023 ESC update):** dapagliflozin/empagliflozin reduce HF hospitalization/CV death.
  - **EMPEROR-Preserved** (empagliflozin, EF >40%, n=5988): primary composite HR **0.79 (0.69–0.90)**, driven by ↓HF hospitalization (HR 0.71) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8587377/)).
  - **DELIVER** (dapagliflozin, EF >40%): primary composite HR **0.82 (0.73–0.92)** ([Frontiers pooled](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2026.1816804/full)).
- **MRA — nuanced (Class 2b):** **TOPCAT** overall neutral, but the **Americas subgroup** showed benefit (regional enrollment/adherence issues in Russia/Georgia) — reasonable to use spironolactone in HFpEF, esp. with elevated NP, EF closer to 50%.
- **Finerenone (nonsteroidal MRA) — FINEARTS-HF (2024):** reduced HF events + CV death in HFmrEF/HFpEF, including on top of SGLT2i — emerging **second pillar** for HFpEF ([ESC](https://www.escardio.org/The-ESC/Press-Office/Press-releases/Finerenone-improves-outcomes-in-a-common-type-of-heart-failure); [CardioNerds](https://www.cardionerds.com/finearts-hf-trial/)).
- **Diuretics** for congestion (Class 1, symptom control).
- **BP control** (Class 1), **AF rate/rhythm control, comorbidity management** (obesity, DM, CAD, CKD, sleep apnea).
- **ARNI/ARB — Class 2b:** candesartan (CHARM-Preserved) and sacubitril/valsartan (PARAGON-HF) showed borderline/nominal benefit, greatest in EF at lower end of preserved range and in women.

**Why HFrEF drugs "failed" in HFpEF:** the dominant lesion is diastolic stiffness/microvascular inflammation, not systolic neurohormonal-driven remodeling — so RAAS/SNS blockade yields little. SGLT2i work via mechanisms (diuresis, energetics, anti-inflammatory) that apply across the EF spectrum.

## 5. Acute Decompensated HF (ADHF) — Wards

### Precipitants — mnemonic "FAILURE"
**F**orgot meds/dietary indiscretion (Na/fluid), **A**rrhythmia (AF/RVR)/**A**nemia, **I**schemia/**I**nfection, **L**ifestyle (Na/fluid excess), **U**pregulation (pregnancy, thyrotoxicosis), **R**enal failure, **E**mbolism (PE)/**E**ndocarditis. Also NSAIDs, negative inotropes (verapamil, some CCBs), uncontrolled HTN, non-adherence.

### Hemodynamic profiles (Stevenson/Forrester — congestion × perfusion)
| | **Warm** (perfused) | **Cold** (hypoperfused) |
|---|---|---|
| **Dry** (no congestion) | A — compensated | L — hypovolemic/over-diuresed |
| **Wet** (congested) | **B — most common ADHF** (diurese ± vasodilate) | **C — worst** (diurese + inotrope ± pressor) |

Signs of **wet:** JVD, rales, edema, orthopnea, S3, ↑weight. Signs of **cold:** narrow pulse pressure, cool extremities, altered mentation, ↓urine output, hyponatremia, rising Cr.

### Diagnostic workup
- **Natriuretic peptides:** **BNP <100** or **NT-proBNP <300** pg/mL → acute HF unlikely (high NPV). Age-stratified NT-proBNP rule-in: **>450 (<50 y), >900 (50–75 y), >1800 (>75 y)** ([ICON-RELOADED-based cutoffs](https://www.droracle.ai/articles/580033/what-are-the-age-specific-nt-probnp-n-terminal-pro-b-type-natriuretic)).
- **Troponin** (ischemia; mild elevation common in ADHF from strain), **BMP** (electrolytes, renal function, guide diuresis), CBC, LFTs (congestive hepatopathy), TSH, iron studies (ferritin/TSAT).
- **CXR:** cephalization, Kerley B lines, perihilar "bat-wing" edema, cardiomegaly, effusions.
- **ECG** (ischemia, arrhythmia), **echo** (EF, valves, filling pressures, wall motion), **POCUS/lung B-lines**.
- **Ischemic workup** if concern for ACS-precipitated HF, new regional wall motion abnormality, or new-onset HFrEF.

### IV diuresis strategy
- **DOSE trial** ([NEJM 2011](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1005419)): 2×2 factorial. **Bolus vs continuous infusion → no difference** in symptoms or renal function. **High-dose (2.5× total daily home oral dose IV) vs low-dose (= home dose IV):** high-dose gave greater diuresis/symptom relief with transient reversible ↑Cr. Practical rule: **give IV furosemide ≈ 2.5× the total daily oral home dose**, q12h bolus, then titrate to urine output/weight.
- **Diuretic resistance:** add a **thiazide** (metolazone 2.5–10 mg, or IV chlorothiazide 500 mg) for **sequential nephron blockade**; watch for profound K⁺/Mg²⁺ loss.
- **Ultrafiltration:** for true diuretic-refractory congestion (CARRESS-HF showed no renal advantage over stepped pharmacologic care — reserve, not routine).

### Vasodilators
- **IV nitroglycerin:** afterload/preload reduction, esp. **hypertensive acute pulmonary edema (SCAPE)** and ongoing ischemia. Start ~10–20 mcg/min, titrate.
- **Nitroprusside:** potent balanced vasodilator for severe HTN/afterload (e.g., acute MR/AR); risk of cyanide/thiocyanate toxicity, coronary steal — needs arterial line.

### Monitoring during admission
- **Daily weights** (same scale, AM), strict **I/O**, daily **BMP** during active diuresis (K⁺, Mg²⁺, Cr), orthostatic assessment, exam for congestion resolution.

### Hold vs continue GDMT during admission
- **Continue** beta-blocker unless overtly cold/hypoperfused/cardiogenic shock (abrupt withdrawal worsens outcomes — B-CONVINCED). Reduce dose rather than stop when possible.
- **Continue ACEi/ARB/ARNI** unless hypotension/AKI/hyperkalemia.
- **SGLT2i:** hold if euglycemic DKA risk (NPO, sepsis) but increasingly continued/initiated in-hospital.
- **Initiate/optimize GDMT before discharge** (Class 1) — hospitalization is the highest-yield window.

## 6. ICU Escalation & Cardiogenic Shock Management

### Triggers for ICU transfer from the floor
- Cardiogenic shock (hypotension + hypoperfusion), rising lactate, need for vasopressors/inotropes
- Respiratory failure needing NIV or intubation
- Refractory pulmonary edema, malignant arrhythmia, need for invasive hemodynamic monitoring
- Worsening end-organ dysfunction (oliguria, ↑Cr, altered mentation, ischemic hepatitis)

### Hemodynamics & invasive monitoring
- **Arterial line** for continuous BP in shock/on vasoactives.
- **PA (Swan-Ganz) catheter:** not routine, but Class 2a in cardiogenic shock with unclear/mixed volume-perfusion status, to guide inotrope/pressor/MCS decisions, and pre-transplant/LVAD evaluation. Cardiogenic shock pattern: ↑PCWP, ↓CI, ↑SVR.

### Vasopressors & inotropes
| Agent | Receptors/mechanism | Typical dose | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Norepinephrine** | α1 >> β1 (vasoconstriction + modest inotropy) | 0.05–0.5+ mcg/kg/min | **First-line vasopressor in cardiogenic shock** |
| **Dopamine** | Dose-dependent: DA→β1→α | 5–20 mcg/kg/min | Avoid — ↑arrhythmia & mortality vs NE in CS |
| **Dobutamine** | β1 >> β2 (inotrope + mild vasodilation) | 2.5–20 mcg/kg/min | First-line **inotrope** for low-output; may ↓BP |
| **Milrinone** | PDE-3 inhibitor → ↑cAMP (inotrope + pulmonary/systemic vasodilator, "inodilator") | 0.125–0.75 mcg/kg/min | Preferred inotrope when **on chronic beta-blocker** (bypasses β-receptor) or **RV failure/pulmonary HTN**; **renally cleared** — reduce in renal dysfunction; longer half-life, more hypotension |
| **Epinephrine** | β1/β2/α (potent) | 0.01–0.5 mcg/kg/min | Refractory shock; ↑lactate, ↑arrhythmia (avoid as first-line — associated with worse outcomes) |
| **Vasopressin** | V1 receptor (non-adrenergic vasoconstriction) | 0.03 U/min (fixed) | Adjunct to NE; useful in vasodilatory component / pulmonary HTN sparing |

**Evidence — NE vs dopamine (SOAP II):** overall neutral mortality but dopamine caused far **more arrhythmias (24.1% vs 12.4%, p<0.001)** and **higher 28-day mortality in the prespecified cardiogenic shock subgroup** ([NEJM/2 Minute Medicine](https://www.2minutemedicine.com/the-soap-ii-trial-first-line-vasopressor-for-shock-management-classics-series/)) → **NE is first-line.**
**Milrinone vs dobutamine (DOREMI, [NEJM 2021](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2026845)):** no significant difference in composite outcome — agent choice is individualized (beta-blocker use, renal function, pulmonary pressures).

### Mechanical Circulatory Support (MCS)
- **IABP:** **IABP-SHOCK II** — no mortality benefit in AMI-cardiogenic shock (30-day 39.7% vs 41.3%; 6-yr 66% vs 67%) → **Class III, routine use not recommended** ([ACC](https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Clinical-Trials/2014/05/14/22/54/IABP-SHOCK-II)).
- **Impella (microaxial flow pump):** **DanGer Shock (2024)** — in **STEMI cardiogenic shock**, Impella CP reduced 180-day mortality **45.8% vs 58.5% (HR 0.74, NNT 8)** but with more complications (bleeding, limb ischemia, renal) ([Wiki Journal Club](https://www.wikijournalclub.org/wiki/DanGer_Shock)). First RCT to show MCS mortality benefit.
- **VA-ECMO:** **ECLS-SHOCK / ECMO-CS** — no survival benefit with routine early ECMO in AMI-CS, high complication rates → reserve for refractory shock/bridge in selected centers.

### Respiratory support
- **NIV (CPAP or BiPAP)** for cardiogenic pulmonary edema: rapidly improves dyspnea, work of breathing, gas exchange, and **reduces intubation rate**. **3CPO trial** ([NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa0707992)) and Cochrane: **no clear mortality benefit** but strong symptomatic/physiologic benefit and ↓intubation — **first-line** for awake, cooperative patients with SCAPE/pulmonary edema. CPAP 5–10 cmH₂O; BiPAP if hypercapnic (PaCO₂ >45).
- **Intubate** if: failure of/contraindication to NIV, obtundation/inability to protect airway, worsening hypercapnia/acidosis, cardiac arrest, refractory hypoxemia.
- **Initial vent settings** (post-intubation, cardiogenic pulmonary edema): volume-control, **Vt 6–8 mL/kg IBW, PEEP 5–10 cmH₂O** (positive intrathoracic pressure reduces preload/afterload — helpful in HF), FiO₂ titrated to SpO₂ ≥92%, RR 14–18. **Caution:** induction agents + positive-pressure ventilation can drop preload/BP abruptly in shock — have vasopressors ready; use hemodynamically stable induction (e.g., etomidate/ketamine), avoid excessive PEEP in RV failure/preload-dependent states.

### ICU de-escalation (downgrade) criteria
- Off or on low, stable vasopressor/inotrope (ideally weaned), normalizing lactate
- Hemodynamically stable, adequate perfusion/urine output
- Off NIV / stable on nasal cannula, or extubated and stable
- Effective transition to oral/stable IV HF regimen, arrhythmia controlled

## 7. Discharge & Disposition

### Discharge readiness criteria (2022 guideline + ESC)
- **Euvolemia** achieved (or near-optimal volume status), congestion resolved
- **Transitioned to oral diuretic** and **stable ≥24 h** on oral regimen
- **Stable renal function and electrolytes** at/near baseline
- No symptomatic hypotension/orthostasis; SBP ≥90; controlled HR
- **GDMT initiated/optimized** and documented; oral regimen tolerated
- Patient/caregiver education (daily weights, Na/fluid restriction, med adherence, when to call), follow-up arranged ([AHA Target:HF discharge checklist](https://www.heart.org/-/media/Files/Professional/Quality-Improvement/Target-Heart-Failure/TargetHF-Discharge-Checklist-ucm_496869.pdf))

### Disposition options
| Setting | Definition | When appropriate |
|---|---|---|
| **Home** | Independent/with family support | Stable, euvolemic, can manage meds/ADLs, reliable follow-up |
| **Home health** | Skilled nursing/PT/OT visits at home | Stable but needs nursing (IV meds, teaching), wound care, or intermittent therapy at home |
| **SNF (skilled nursing facility)** | Skilled nursing + lower-intensity rehab (<3 h/day); Medicare covers up to **100 days after qualifying 3-day inpatient stay** | Deconditioned/needs subacute rehab or nursing but cannot tolerate intensive therapy |
| **IRF (acute inpatient rehab)** | Intensive multidisciplinary rehab, **~3 h therapy/day, ≥5 days/week**, physician oversight | Good rehab potential, can tolerate intensive therapy, needs ≥2 disciplines ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/training-education/medicare-learning-networkr-mln/compliance/medicare-provider-compliance-tips/inpatient-rehabilitation-hospitals)) |
| **LTAC (long-term acute care hospital)** | Hospital-level care, **avg LOS >25 days** | Prolonged complex needs — vent weaning, complex wounds, prolonged inotrope dependence ([PAM Health](https://pamhealth.com/resources/differences-between-ltachs-irfs-and-snfs/)) |
| **Palliative / hospice** | Symptom-focused; hospice = prognosis ≤6 mo, comfort-directed | Advanced (Stage D) HF, refractory symptoms, recurrent admissions, declined/ineligible for advanced therapies |

### Transitions of care
- **Early follow-up within 7 days** of discharge (Class 1) — reduces readmission.
- Rapid GDMT up-titration per **STRONG-HF** model with close post-discharge labs/visits.
- **Cardiac rehabilitation / exercise training:** Class 1 in stable HF — improves functional capacity, QoL, and reduces hospitalization.

## 8. Clinic / Outpatient Context

### Chronic optimization
- **HFrEF:** maintain/titrate all four pillars to target or maximally tolerated doses; add hydralazine/ISDN, ivabradine, vericiguat, digoxin, devices as indicated.
- **HFpEF:** SGLT2i (Class 1), consider MRA/finerenone, aggressive BP/comorbidity control, diuretic for congestion.

### Monitoring cadence
- After starting/uptitrating **ACEi/ARB/ARNI or MRA:** check **BMP (K⁺, Cr) in 1–2 weeks** (MRA: also ~day 3 and day 7), then periodically.
- **SGLT2i:** monitor volume status/renal function; expect small initial eGFR dip (reversible).
- Daily home **weights** (call for gain >2–3 lb/day or 5 lb/week), symptom/Na/fluid diaries.
- Periodic NP trend, echo for EF reassessment (esp. before ICD/CRT decisions after ≥3 mo GDMT).

### USPSTF Grade A/B recommendations relevant to the HF/cardiac patient
- **Hypertension screening — Grade A:** all adults ≥18 y, with office BP confirmed outside clinical setting before diagnosis ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/hypertension-in-adults-screening)).
- **Statin for primary prevention of CVD — Grade B:** adults **40–75 y with ≥1 CVD risk factor (dyslipidemia, DM, HTN, smoking) and estimated 10-yr risk ≥10%** ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/statin-use-in-adults-preventive-medication)).
- **AAA screening — Grade B:** one-time ultrasonography in **men 65–75 y who have ever smoked** ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/abdominal-aortic-aneurysm-screening)).
- **Tobacco smoking cessation — Grade A:** ask all adults about tobacco use; provide behavioral interventions + FDA-approved pharmacotherapy ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/tobacco-use-in-adults-and-pregnant-women-counseling-and-interventions)).
- **Prediabetes/Type 2 diabetes screening — Grade B:** adults **35–70 y with overweight/obesity**; refer those with prediabetes to preventive interventions ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-for-prediabetes-and-type-2-diabetes)).
- (Also relevant: healthy-diet/physical-activity counseling — Grade B for adults with CVD risk factors.)

### Vaccinations & comorbidity management
- **Vaccinate:** annual influenza, pneumococcal, COVID-19, RSV (age-appropriate) — Class 1, reduce HF exacerbations.
- **AF:** rate/rhythm control + anticoagulation per CHA₂DS₂-VASc; **catheter ablation** reasonable in HFrEF + AF (CASTLE-AF).
- **HTN:** target <130/80.
- **DM:** prefer **SGLT2i** (dual HF benefit); metformin safe in stable HF.
- **CKD:** SGLT2i, finerenone; watch K⁺ with RAAS/MRA.
- **Sleep apnea:** treat OSA; **avoid adaptive servo-ventilation in HFrEF with predominant central sleep apnea** (SERVE-HF — ↑mortality).
- **Iron deficiency (ferritin <100, or 100–299 with TSAT <20%):** **IV ferric carboxymaltose** improves symptoms/QoL and reduces HF hospitalization (**AFFIRM-AHF, IRONMAN**); **HEART-FID** neutral on hierarchical composite. 2022 AHA = Class 2a for QoL/functional status; **oral iron ineffective (IRONOUT-HF)** ([ACC review](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2024/08/01/01/42/focus-on-heart-failure-ironclad); [IRONMAN, Lancet](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/piis0140-6736(22)02083-9/fulltext)).

### Red flags → ED / admission from clinic
- Rest dyspnea, hypoxia, acute pulmonary edema
- Hypotension + hypoperfusion (cool, confused, oliguric) — cardiogenic shock
- Chest pain / suspected ACS, syncope, sustained/symptomatic arrhythmia
- Rapid weight gain with refractory congestion despite oral diuretic uptitration
- Symptomatic hyponatremia, severe hyper/hypokalemia, acute kidney injury

## 10. High-Yield Clinical Pearls & Decision Points

1. **Get all four pillars on early — don't max one before adding the next.** Low-dose quadruple therapy beats high-dose partial therapy. Start SGLT2i and MRA early; they don't need slow titration.
2. **ARNI: honor the 36-hour ACEi washout** — angioedema risk. No washout from an ARB.
3. **Don't stop the beta-blocker in decompensated HF unless the patient is cold/hypoperfused** — reduce the dose instead (B-CONVINCED). Do NOT initiate a beta-blocker in a "wet" unstable patient — wait until euvolemic.
4. **IV loop diuretic dose ≈ 2.5× total daily home oral dose** (DOSE). If oral bioavailability is a concern (gut edema), IV is more reliable; furosemide PO→IV is ~2:1.
5. **Diuretic resistance → add a thiazide (metolazone) for sequential nephron blockade** — but watch K⁺/Mg²⁺ closely.
6. **A transient ↑Cr during effective decongestion is acceptable** — don't reflexively stop diuresis if the patient is still congested and otherwise stable.
7. **MRA gate: eGFR >30 and K⁺ <5.0 to start; stop if K⁺ can't stay <5.5.** Recheck labs at ~day 3 and day 7.
8. **Norepinephrine is the first-line pressor in cardiogenic shock** (dopamine → more arrhythmia and worse CS mortality, SOAP II).
9. **Milrinone over dobutamine when the patient is on a chronic beta-blocker** (PDE-3 bypasses the blocked β-receptor) or has RV failure/pulmonary HTN — but it's renally cleared and more hypotensive.
10. **NIV (CPAP/BiPAP) for flash pulmonary edema** reduces work of breathing and intubation even though it doesn't clearly cut mortality (3CPO) — use it early in the awake patient.
11. **IABP is not routine in cardiogenic shock (Class III);** Impella has a mortality signal in STEMI-CS (DanGer Shock) but real bleeding/limb/renal complications — patient selection matters.
12. **Continue GDMT indefinitely in HFimpEF even after EF normalizes** (TRED-HF: withdrawal → relapse).
13. **SGLT2i for HFpEF is Class 1** — the one drug class that clearly works across the EF spectrum; add finerenone as an emerging second option.
14. **Oral iron doesn't work in HF (IRONOUT-HF)** — use **IV** iron for iron deficiency; check ferritin + TSAT even if not anemic.
15. **Avoid in HFrEF:** NSAIDs, non-dihydropyridine CCBs (verapamil/diltiazem), thiazolidinediones (fluid retention), and most antiarrhythmics except amiodarone/dofetilide.
16. **Sacubitril/valsartan and ACEi/ARB can cause a false-normal or altered BNP interpretation** — neprilysin inhibition raises BNP but not NT-proBNP; **use NT-proBNP to track patients on ARNI.**
17. **Early 7-day post-discharge follow-up + rapid uptitration (STRONG-HF)** is the single highest-yield intervention to prevent readmission.
18. **Wait ≥40 days post-MI and ≥90 days on GDMT before reassessing EF for a primary-prevention ICD** — many EFs recover with medical therapy.

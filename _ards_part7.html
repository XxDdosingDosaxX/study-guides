Fourteen things worth carrying into the unit

  1. The commonest error in ARDS is not calling it. LUNG SAFE found clinicians recognised ARDS in only ~60% of patients who met the criteria — and only 51% of mild cases. Every therapy that works depends on someone first saying the word out loud. Apply the four Berlin criteria deliberately, to every patient with bilateral infiltrates and hypoxaemia.
  2. You cannot oxygenate your way out of a shunt. At a 30% shunt, 100% oxygen buys a PaO₂ of ~100; at 50%, ~49. If the saturation barely moves on a non-rebreather, stop turning up the FiO₂ and start thinking about PEEP, prone and the tube.
  3. Dose tidal volume on PREDICTED body weight — from height and sex. Never actual weight. This is the commonest lethal ventilator error in general ICUs, and it is committed by people who "know" about lung-protective ventilation. Measure the height.
  4. Driving pressure (Pplat − PEEP) is the number that individualises the protocol. 6 mL/kg is a population rule; ΔP < 15 is that rule applied to this lung. If ΔP is high, you are over-stretching this patient regardless of what the mL/kg says.
  5. Prone early, prone long, and don't stop because the PaO₂ didn't improve. P/F < 150 → ≥ 16 h/day, within 36–48 h. The mortality benefit comes from more homogeneous ventilation (less VILI), not from better oxygenation — so oxygenation non-responders benefit too.
  6. A "normal" PaCO₂ in a patient breathing 35 times a minute is a pre-arrest sign. They should be at 25. A CO₂ drifting up toward normal means the pump is failing. This is the most dangerous falsely-reassuring number in medicine.
  7. The most dangerous patient on the ward is the one on HFNC or NIV who "looks comfortable." Both make failure look like success. Set a numeric deadline in advance (ROX ≥ 4.88 at 6 h; NIV improving pH and CO₂ at 1–2 h) and honour it. Delayed intubation after failed non-invasive support increases mortality.
  8. Resuscitate → stabilise → de-resuscitate, in that order. Give the fluid the shocked patient needs, then stop, then take it off. FACTT bought 2.5 ventilator-free days with no extra shock or dialysis. The error is never getting to phase three.
  9. Steroids in ARDS are an EARLY drug. Dexamethasone 20 mg × 5 d then 10 mg × 5 d (DEXA-ARDS) or 6 mg × 10 d (COVID). Started after 14 days, steroids were associated with increased mortality (LaSRS). And always exclude an undrained source first.
  10. A sudden desaturation on the vent is DOPES until proven otherwise — Displaced tube, Obstructed tube, Pneumothorax, Equipment failure, Stacked breaths. People die of a mucus plug while their team arranges ECMO.
  11. Call ECMO before you need it. The window closes at ~7 days of high-setting ventilation — which is exactly the period you spend "waiting to see." A phone call costs nothing.
  12. The right ventricle is what kills them — and every ARDS therapy attacks it. Hypoxaemia, hypercapnia, acidosis and high PEEP all raise pulmonary vascular resistance. If the RV fails: echo, limit ΔP, ease off PEEP, relax the permissive hypercapnia, prone, and use norepinephrine/vasopressin (not agents that raise PVR).
  13. Check the phosphate and the magnesium before you blame the lung. A hypophosphataemic diaphragm cannot pass an SBT, and nobody will work out why.
  14. The disability of ARDS is not pulmonary. At five years, survivors' lungs are largely normal and their lives are not: weakness, cognitive impairment and PTSD dominate. Which means the sedation you give today, and the mobility you do or don't do today, is the disability you cause a year from now. Light sedation, no benzodiazepines, early mobility, A–F bundle. Every day.

The landmark trials — what each one actually changed

TrialYearQuestionResultWhy it matters
ARMA (ARDSNet)
NEJM 2000		20006 vs 12 mL/kg PBW tidal volume in ARDSMortality 31.0% vs 39.8% (p = 0.007). Stopped early for benefit.The foundation of everything. Established that the ventilator itself was killing patients, and that a smaller breath saved them. The single largest mortality benefit of any ARDS therapy, and the reason "6 mL/kg PBW" is a reflex.
ALVEOLI
NEJM 2004		2004Higher vs lower PEEP (on top of low VT)No mortality difference.Killed the idea that more PEEP is simply better for everyone. Later meta-analysis (Briel 2010) suggested benefit confined to moderate–severe ARDS — which is why we now use the high-PEEP table selectively rather than universally.
FACTT (ARDSNet)
NEJM 2006		2006Conservative vs liberal fluid strategyNo mortality difference, but +2.5 ventilator-free days (14.6 vs 12.1) and better oxygenation — with no increase in shock or dialysis.Made "dry lungs" a therapeutic goal. The evidence base for de-resuscitating the patient once shock has resolved.
LaSRS (ARDSNet)
NEJM 2006		2006Methylprednisolone in persistent ARDSNo overall benefit; started after 14 days → INCREASED 60-day mortality. More neuromuscular weakness.The reason steroid timing matters. The single most important negative steroid trial in ARDS.
ACURASYS
NEJM 2010		2010Early cisatracurium × 48 h in P/F < 150Improved adjusted 90-day survival; ~9% absolute reduction in 28-day mortality. No increase in ICU-acquired weakness.Made early paralysis standard — for a decade. Control arm was deeply sedated.
OSCILLATE / OSCAR
NEJM 2013		2013High-frequency oscillatory ventilation (HFOV)OSCILLATE stopped early for HARM (in-hospital mortality 47% vs 35%). OSCAR neutral.Removed HFOV from the rescue algorithm entirely. A cautionary tale about physiologically seductive therapies.
PROSEVA
NEJM 2013		2013Prone ≥ 16 h/day in severe ARDS (P/F < 150)28-day mortality 16.0% vs 32.8% (HR 0.39); 90-day 23.6% vs 41.0%. NNT ≈ 6.One of the largest absolute mortality reductions in all of critical care, from a free intervention requiring only people and time. The reason proning is not optional in severe ARDS.
FLORALI
NEJM 2015		2015HFNC vs NIV vs conventional O₂ in acute hypoxaemic (non-hypercapnic) failureNo difference in the primary intubation endpoint overall; HFNC reduced 90-day mortality, and reduced intubation in the pre-specified P/F ≤ 200 subgroup.Established HFNC as the first-line non-invasive support for type 1 failure — and raised real concern about NIV in this population.
Amato — driving pressure
NEJM 2015		2015Mediation analysis, 3,562 patients, 9 trialsDriving pressure was the ventilator variable most strongly associated with survival — and changes in VT/PEEP only helped when they lowered ΔP.Reframed lung-protective ventilation around ΔP < 15 rather than mL/kg alone.
LUNG SAFE
JAMA 2016		2016Global epidemiology of ARDS (50 countries)ARDS in 10.4% of ICU admissions and 23.4% of ventilated patients. Recognised by clinicians in only ~60%. Hospital mortality 34.9% (mild) → 40.3% (moderate) → 46.1% (severe).The under-recognition statistic that should change your practice today. Also showed lung-protective ventilation was widely under-applied.
ART
JAMA 2017		2017Lung recruitment manoeuvre + PEEP titration vs low PEEPINCREASED 28-day mortality (55.3% vs 49.3%) and 6-month mortality; more barotrauma and hypotension.Killed aggressive recruitment manoeuvres. Both ATS and ESICM now recommend strongly against prolonged RMs.
EOLIA
NEJM 2018		2018Early VV-ECMO in very severe ARDS60-day mortality 35% vs 46%, p = 0.09 — formally negative. But 28% of controls crossed over to rescue ECMO. Bayesian re-analyses favour benefit.Defined the ECMO referral criteria still used today. A masterclass in why "negative" is not the same as "no effect."
ROSE (PETAL)
NEJM 2019		2019Re-test of early cisatracurium — but with a lightly sedated, high-PEEP control armNo difference — 90-day mortality 42.5% vs 42.8%.Overturned routine paralysis. Reconciles with ACURASYS via the control arm: paralysis helps only when spontaneous effort is doing harm. Source of the ATS/ESICM disagreement.
DEXA-ARDS
Lancet Respir Med 2020		2020Dexamethasone 20 mg × 5 d → 10 mg × 5 d in early moderate–severe ARDS60-day mortality 21% vs 36%; ventilator-free days 12.3 vs 7.5.The strongest non-COVID steroid signal in ARDS. Caveat, stated plainly: a single, unblinded, early-stopped trial in a narrow population. Promising, not definitive.
RECOVERY (dexamethasone)
NEJM 2021		2021Dexamethasone 6 mg daily × 10 d in COVID-19Reduced 28-day mortality — benefit confined to patients receiving oxygen or ventilation; a signal of harm in those needing no oxygen.The clearest steroid indication in respiratory failure — and a sharp reminder that the same drug can help the hypoxaemic and harm the well.

Guidelines & definitions

  • ATS 2024 — the current ARDS guideline: Qiu H, Fan E, Brodie D, et al. "An Update on Management of Adult Patients with Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome: An Official American Thoracic Society Clinical Practice Guideline." Am J Respir Crit Care Med. 2024;209(1):24–36. PMC10870893 · PubMed
  • ESICM 2023 ARDS guideline: Grasselli G, Calfee CS, Camporota L, et al. "ESICM guidelines on acute respiratory distress syndrome: definition, phenotyping and respiratory support strategies." Intensive Care Med. 2023;49(7):727–759. Springer · PubMed
  • Where the two guidelines disagree (and why): "Mind the gap: understanding the discordance between the ATS and ESICM ARDS guidelines." Intensive Care Med. 2024. Springer · Bedside application: PMC12040422
  • New Global Definition of ARDS (2024): Matthay MA, Arabi Y, Arroliga AC, et al. Am J Respir Crit Care Med. 2024;209(1):37–47. PMC10870872 · PubMed
  • Berlin Definition: ARDS Definition Task Force. "Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome: The Berlin Definition." JAMA. 2012;307(23):2526–2533. PubMed
  • ARDSNet / NHLBI ventilator protocol card (PBW formulas, both PEEP/FiO₂ tables, the plateau and pH algorithms, weaning criteria): ardsnet.org — ventilator protocol (PDF)
  • Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021 (MAP target, vasopressor sequence, steroid indication): Evans L, et al. Crit Care Med. 2021;49(11):e1063–e1143. PMC8486643
  • PADIS 2018 (pain, agitation, delirium, immobility, sleep — the basis of light sedation and the A–F bundle): Devlin JW, et al. Crit Care Med. 2018;46(9):e825–e873.
  • USPSTF A & B recommendations (tobacco cessation, lung cancer screening, unhealthy alcohol use, obesity, HIV): uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org

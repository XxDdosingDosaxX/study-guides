The evidence base — and the honest state of it

Read this before you trust any protocol, including this one. Rhabdomyolysis has never had a randomised controlled trial of fluid ± bicarbonate ± mannitol, and there is no Cochrane review. Both GRADE-based society guidelines — EAST 2022 and DASAIM/DSIT 2019 — could issue only conditional/weak recommendations, on low or very low quality evidence.

So bicarbonate and mannitol are unproven, not disproven — which is precisely why they refuse to die, and why two attendings will tell you two different things. But note what the two guidelines agree on, independently: aggressive early IV fluid helps; bicarbonate and mannitol do not. That convergence is as close to a settled answer as this disease offers.

What is genuinely well-supported: give isotonic fluid, give it early, titrate it to urine output, remove the trigger, and watch the potassium and the limb. Everything else is contested — and this guide flags the conflicts rather than inventing a consensus that does not exist.

Key trials & landmark evidence

StudyYearDesign / nResultWhy it matters
Bywaters & Beall
Br Med J		1941 Case series — victims of the London Blitz First description of crush syndrome: limb compression → dark urine → renal failure → death. The paper that created the disease. Everything since is a footnote to it.
Ron et al.
Arch Intern Med		1984 Case series — 7 crush victims, building collapse Early mannitol-alkaline forced diuresis, started at the scene: no patient developed renal failure. Established the doctrine of very early, aggressive fluid — the single most durable idea in this disease. (Its mannitol/bicarbonate component is what later evidence undermined; the early fluid component survived.)
Brown CV, et al.
J Trauma		2004 Retrospective, trauma ICU, n = 2,083 In patients with CK >5,000 U/L, bicarbonate + mannitol produced NO difference in renal failure, dialysis, or mortality. Also: CK >5,000 → 19% renal failure vs 8% below. The study that broke the dogma. Its conclusion — "the standard of administering BIC/MAN… should be reevaluated" — is why most protocols today are fluid-only.
Sever & Vanholder (ISN RDRTF)
Nephrol Dial Transplant		2012 Consensus recommendations + earthquake registries (>1,200 dialysed) The definitive crush protocol: isotonic saline 1 L/h before extrication; ≤500 mL/h if entrapment >2 h; 3–6 L in the first 6 h; avoid potassium-containing fluids; no NSAIDs; mannitol only after a test dose. The operational manual for the deadliest form of this disease — and the source of the "treat before you extricate" principle.
McMahon GM, Zeng X, Waikar SS
JAMA Intern Med		2013 Derivation + validation cohort, n = 2,371 (CPK >5,000) Derived the McMahon score. AUC 0.83. Score <5 → 2.3% risk of death/RRT; >10 → 61.2%. NPV 97% at a cutoff of 5. The only validated risk tool in rhabdomyolysis — and a formal statement that CK is a weak predictor (CK appears once, worth 2 points, and only above 40,000; aetiology is worth 3).
Scharman & Troutman
Ann Pharmacother		2013 Systematic review, 27 studies Fluid within 6 h; urine output ≥300 mL/h for ≥24 h. Bicarbonate only to correct a systemic acidosis; mannitol only if urine output stays <300 mL/h despite adequate fluid. 8 studies showed delayed fluid → more AKI. The cleanest synthesis of the fluid literature, and the source of the "within 6 hours" target.
Simpson JP, et al.
J Intensive Care Soc (10-yr cohort)		2016 External validation, n = 232 McMahon ≥6 predicted RRT with sensitivity 86% / specificity 68% — beating peak CK ≥5,000 (83% / 55%). Validated the score against the number everyone actually quotes on rounds — and the score won.
Chavez LO, et al.
Crit Care		2016 Systematic review No RCTs exist comparing fluid alone vs fluid + bicarbonate/mannitol. Avoid calcium for asymptomatic hypocalcemia. CRRT improves myoglobin clearance but mortality is unchanged. The source of the "don't give calcium" and "don't dialyse the myoglobin" rules.
Nelson DA, et al.
NEJM		2016 Cohort, n = 47,944 US Army soldiers Sickle cell trait raised rhabdomyolysis risk (HR 1.54, 95% CI 1.12–2.12) — but mortality was NO different (HR 0.99). Confirms sickle cell trait as a real risk factor and argues against screening-and-exclusion policies. Counsel and mitigate; don't disqualify.
Michelsen J, et al. (DASAIM/DSIT)
Acta Anaesthesiol Scand		2019 GRADE clinical practice guideline FOR early (rather than late) fluid; crystalloid over colloid. AGAINST routine mannitol, loop diuretics, sodium bicarbonate, alkalinisation, and routine RRT. All eight recommendations weak; evidence low/very low. The GRADE-based Scandinavian guideline on preventing rhabdomyolysis-induced AKI. It contradicts a lot of what is still taught.
EAST practice management guideline
Am J Surg		2022 GRADE clinical practice guideline (Eastern Association for the Surgery of Trauma; 12 studies) Conditionally FOR aggressive IV fluid resuscitation — it reduced acute renal failure and the need for dialysis. Conditionally AGAINST bicarbonate and conditionally AGAINST mannitol — neither improved renal failure or dialysis rates. The North American society guideline — and the single most useful citation in this topic. Written independently, on a different literature set, from a different continent, it reaches exactly the same conclusions as DASAIM/DSIT 2019. When two GRADE panels converge like this, you can stop arguing: fluid yes, bicarbonate and mannitol no.
ACSM Clinical Practice Guidelines for Exertional Rhabdomyolysis
Curr Sports Med Rep		2021 Clinical practice guideline (military medicine perspective) Staged 4-phase return to activity: minimum 72 h rest; advance only when CK <5× ULN, urinalysis normal, and asymptomatic. The operational answer to "when can I train again?" — the question every exertional case will ask you.
French multicentre ICU cohort
Ann Intensive Care		2020 Retrospective, severe rhabdomyolysis in ICU, n = 387 81.4% developed AKI; 44.1% KDIGO Stage 3; 26.6% needed RRT. Severe AKI correlated with admission phosphate, potassium, bicarbonate and myoglobin — but NOT with CK. Quantifies just how bad ICU-grade rhabdomyolysis is — and lands the central lesson again: the CK is not the risk.
"Urinalysis is an inadequate screen for rhabdomyolysis"
Am J Emerg Med		2014 Diagnostic accuracy study Heme-positive dipstick with no RBCs: sensitivity only 41% (and only 55% even when CK ≥10,000). A negative dipstick does NOT rule out rhabdomyolysis. The classic teaching is a good rule-in sign and a terrible rule-out test. Send a CK.
CLEAR Outcomes (bempedoic acid)
NEJM		2023 RCT, statin-intolerant patients Bempedoic acid reduced major cardiovascular events in patients unable or unwilling to take statins. The outcome evidence behind the non-statin arm of the SAMS ladder — and mechanistically elegant, because bempedoic acid is a prodrug activated only in liver, not in muscle.

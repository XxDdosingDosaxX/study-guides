Why turning up the FiO₂ stops working. Curves computed from the shunt equation (alveolar gas equation with PB 760, PaCO₂ 40, R 0.8; Hb 15 g/dL; C(a−v)O₂ 5 mL/dL; Severinghaus dissociation curve). With healthy lungs (green) oxygen is a lever. Once a third of the cardiac output is running past flooded, unventilated alveoli, the lever breaks — the blood in the shunt never meets gas, so no amount of gas in the other alveoli can fix it. This is the entire reason ARDS is treated with PEEP and prone positioning (which re-open alveoli) rather than with more oxygen.
The three units. Hypoxemia has five mechanisms, but only two matter at the bedside in acute respiratory failure — and they behave completely differently when you turn up the oxygen. V̇/Q̇ mismatch responds to FiO₂; true shunt does not. The bedside test: put the patient on a non-rebreather. If the saturation barely moves, you are looking at shunt, and you are looking at ARDS until proven otherwise.
The baby lung (Gattinoni). The ARDS chest is normal-sized, but only a fraction of it is still aerated — CT-quantified, the aerated compartment is often the size of a 5-year-old's lung. The ventilator does not know this. A "normal" 500 mL breath is not normal when it is being forced into a litre of lung; it is a 500 mL breath into a child. That is the entire argument for 6 mL/kg: you are not ventilating a small person, you are ventilating a small lung. Driving pressure (ΔP = Pplat − PEEP = VT/Crs) is the number that actually normalises tidal volume to the size of the lung that is left — which is why it tracks mortality better than VT alone (Amato, NEJM 2015).
Why prone works — and why it is not just "turning the patient over." Perfusion in the lung is anatomically dorsal-predominant and stays roughly dorsal whichever way you flip the patient; ventilation is not — it follows gravity. Supine, the dorsal lung is crushed by the heart, the abdomen and the weight of the oedematous lung above it, so the best-perfused region is the least-ventilated: a large shunt. Prone, the dorsal lung re-opens, the heart falls forward onto the sternum instead of onto lung, the pleural-pressure gradient flattens, and ventilation is finally delivered where the blood already is. PROSEVA (NEJM 2013) proned severe ARDS (P/F < 150) for ≥16 h/day and cut 28-day mortality from 32.8% to 16.0% — one of the largest absolute mortality reductions of any intervention in critical care.
